## Operator design direction — now authorized for bounded No14 candidate

The optimization goal is substantial compression that substantially improves readability, rather than merely exposing a musical shape or mandating absolute melody notation. Chords and arpeggios are conservative initial opportunities: one absolute played reference plus relative component symbols can expose recurring structures across transpositions. Dense chords may offer particularly large reading gains, including extensions, inversions and doubled tones; retain their actual voicing/register and all independent durations.

Candidate group identity should consider the intersection of musical sound grouping, performing-hand shape and rhythmic/metrical grouping. These often coincide, but none alone is universal: a beam is not automatic evidence of an arpeggio, printed voice is not performing hand, and inferred harmony is not a source fact. Keep this relationship in mind when reviewing future rhythmic-grouping decisions. The operator subsequently authorized a bounded No14 source-derived grouping candidate and relative parity placement. A universal recognition algorithm and claims of reading gains remain outside this implementation.

Current engine distinction: Brahms uses absolute even/odd parity columns; No14 and global defaults use standard collision placement. Per-hand clasp admission is a physical duration/grouping rule and does not define every chord or relative-reading group; qualifying overlapping hands may share an outer clasp with independent duration groups. For a relative-parity silhouette, subtracting an odd anchor exchanges the absolute parity families. Changing labels alone therefore does not guarantee a transposition-stable two-column shape. The operator explicitly settled this: relative groups use relative parity. The absolute source register remains intact.
