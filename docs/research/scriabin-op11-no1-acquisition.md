## Decision and stop gate

**Recommendation, not final selection:** Ask the operator whether **Henle HN 484** should be the critical comparison target and whether legitimate full-score access is available. Retain the identified **1897 first edition** as historical witness; evaluate **Snortum No. 1** as a potentially convenient starter, with Mysterium and Mutopia as discrepancy detectors only. Henle's [Urtext guidance](https://www.henle.de/en/What-is-Urtext/) explains why editorial source evaluation and choices matter beyond a label. No candidate was certified as an edition-faithful replica. No vetted MusicXML emerged **in this bounded search**; that is not a claim none exists.

The pilot searched Henle/critical-edition and publisher terms, IMSLP/Belaieff, Mutopia, GitHub LilyPond and **kern, and MusicXML/MIDI terms. Inspectable apparatus and source files yielded a viable route with material unknowns; further web surveying would not resolve the edition/access choice. IMSLP's usual file link led to an access test; only a mirror range request succeeded. No full modern score or complete historical scan was inspected. Before any import, sample No. 1's beginning, ending, texture changes and apparatus-flagged passages against the chosen edition; **later full-score certification** must check pitches/spelling, voices/rests, ties/tuplets/rhythm, expression, variants and completeness. Visual/layout fidelity is separate. Unresolved editorial readings require human judgment, not a majority of derived encodings. No purchase, compilation, acquisition or import was performed. **Visual impact: not affected** — prose/skill only; no served engraving was changed.
